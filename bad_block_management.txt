In the context of NVMe SSDs, **Grown Bad Blocks (GBB)**—often referred to as **Reallocated Sectors**—are NAND flash memory cells that were functional when the drive left the factory but became unreliable or unusable during the drive's operation.

Unlike "Factory Bad Blocks" (which are identified and mapped out during the manufacturing process), Grown Bad Blocks "grow" over time due to the natural wear and tear of flash memory.

### How Grown Bad Blocks Occur

NAND flash memory has a limited lifespan measured in **Program/Erase (P/E) cycles**. Each time data is written or erased, the insulating oxide layer of the cell degrades slightly. Eventually:

1. **Wear-out:** The cell can no longer hold a charge reliably.
2. **Bit Errors:** The drive detects an uncorrectable number of bit errors (ECC failure) when trying to read that specific block.
3. **Write/Erase Failure:** The NVMe controller attempts to write data or clear a block, and the operation fails.

### How the NVMe Controller Handles GBB

The NVMe controller is designed to handle these failures silently through a process called **Bad Block Management**:

* **Retirement:** Once a block is flagged as "bad," the controller marks it as unusable to prevent any future data from being stored there.
* **Reallocation:** The controller takes data that was supposed to go to the bad block and moves it to a "Spare Area"—a hidden reserve of healthy NAND blocks set aside specifically for this purpose.
* **Mapping:** The drive updates its internal logical-to-physical mapping table so the Operating System never knows a physical shift occurred.

### Why It Matters

* **Drive Health (SMART):** You can monitor the "growth" of these blocks using **SMART** (Self-Monitoring, Analysis, and Reporting Technology) attributes. In NVMe, look for the **Percentage Used** and **Available Spare** attributes.
* **Performance:** If a drive starts generating GBBs rapidly, it may indicate a failing NAND chip. While the drive can handle a few, a sudden spike usually suggests the drive is approaching its **End of Life (EOL)**.
* **Data Integrity:** Modern controllers use strong **ECC (Error Correction Code)** to recover data from a block just before it goes "bad," ensuring you don't lose files during the transition to a spare block.

### Summary Table

| Feature | Factory Bad Blocks | Grown Bad Blocks (GBB) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Origin** | Manufacturing defects | Physical wear / P/E cycles |
| **Detection** | During factory testing | During active use (Read/Write) |
| **Solution** | Mapped out before shipping | Reallocated to "Spare Area" |
| **User Impact** | None | Visible in SMART health reports |

If you notice the "Available Spare" percentage dropping significantly in your NVMe utility, it is a sign that the GBB count is rising and it is time to back up your data.
